We are now working towards a production-level GF2 calculation.
There are several choices that we could make that affect numerical behavior, convergence, and implementation.
The first is the choice of spatial basis: we can represent Green's functions in the AO basis (Identity becomes an overlap matrix), MO basis (Identity is true identity matrix, but every ao axis gets multiplied by MO coefficients) , or canonicalized AO basis (Multiply each ao axis by matrix square root of overlap).
For this story, we will implement the noninteracting Green's function guess in the regular AO basis (it would, however, be nice to have some abstraction over these different bases. Maybe a set of matrices to define the Identity and Basis transformation matrices are sufficient? Either way, this abstraction should exist as a layer on top of raw Tensor storage.)

Implementing the guess is the first step in a self-consistent GF2 calculation, and is the objective of this Story.


## Dyson equation
The Dyson equation is point-local in Matsubara frequency space, but a convolution in tau-space.
It makes much more sense to be in Matsubara frequency space to solve this equation.


## The GF2 self-consistent loop
0. Form the noninteracting Green's function guess over Fermionic Matsubara frequencies.
```text
    G(iω_n) = [(iω_n + μ)*S - H_0 - Σ(iω_n) - Σ_HF]^-1
```
Here, S is the overlap matrix, μ is the chemical potential (a scalar), H_0 is the core Hamiltonian in AO indices, Σ(iω_n) is frequency-dependent part of the self-energy (0 for the guess, will be nonzero on future iterations), and finally Σ_HF is the frequency-independent self-energy.
The overlap matrix S, the core Hamiltonian H_0, and Σ_HF are all raw Tensor objects: no frequency-dependent expansion. They are essentially constants in time/frequency space.
However, the Green's function G and the frequency-dependent self-energy Σ do have Fermionic Matsubara frequency expansions.

The Core Hamiltonian and overlap matrix are read in from the input file and are prerequisites for a GF2 calculation.
The density matrix P is another prerequisite (a two-index AO frequency-independent matrix), it is used to get the chemical potential by evaluating
```text
μ=Tr(PS)
```
where Tr is the matrix trace.
This number should match the number of electrons in the system.
The density matrix P as well as the 3-index ERI tensor B^Q_{uv} (Q: RI dimension, uv: AO dimensions) is also used to solve for the frequency-independent self energy Σ_HF:
```text
Σ_HF_{uv} = \sum_Q B^Q_{uv} ρ_Q - 0.5 * \sum_{Q k l} B^Q_{u l} P_{k l} B^Q_{k v}
```
where 
```text
ρ_Q = \sum_{k l} B^Q_{k l} P_{k l}
```
The calculation of the frequency-independent self energy should be made into a function so that it may be easily called repeatedly, as the density matrix P will change during the self-consistent calculation.

As for solving the Green's function, instead of computing the matrix inverse, solve the equivalent expression
```text
    [(iω_n + μ)*S - H_0 - Σ(iω_n) - Σ_HF] G(iω_n) = I
```
This avoids potentially numerically unstable matrix inversion.


## Pitfalls
High-frequency tail expansion for matrix-valued objects is different depending on the basis (because of the differing identity matrices)
